1. How does the directive define statutory interest for late payment?

   Answer: It defines "statutory interest for late payment" as **simple interest** at
   a rate equal to **the reference rate plus at least eight percentage points**. The
   reference rate is, for a euro-area Member State, the rate applied by the European
   Central Bank to its most recent main refinancing operations (or the marginal rate
   from the most recent variable-rate tender for those operations); for a Member
   State outside the euro it is the equivalent rate set by its national central bank.
   Source 1, Article 2(6) and Article 2(7).

2. The owner's note says the EU maximum late fee is 25 EUR. Is that right?

   Answer: No. The directive does not set a maximum late fee of 25 EUR. Where late
   payment interest becomes payable, the creditor is entitled to obtain from the
   debtor, **as a minimum, a fixed sum of EUR 40**, payable **without a reminder** and
   as compensation for the creditor's own recovery costs. On top of that fixed sum,
   the creditor may claim reasonable compensation for any recovery costs exceeding
   40 EUR, such as instructing a lawyer or a debt collection agency. So the figure in
   the directive is a **floor of 40 EUR**, not a 25 EUR ceiling. Source 1, Article 6(1),
   6(2) and 6(3).

3. Does the business have to send a reminder before it is entitled to interest and to
   the fixed compensation sum?

   Answer: No. In commercial transactions between undertakings the creditor is
   entitled to **interest for late payment without the necessity of a reminder** once
   the conditions are met (the creditor has fulfilled its obligations and has not
   received the amount due on time, unless the debtor is not responsible for the
   delay). The fixed compensation sum is likewise **payable without the necessity of
   a reminder**. Source 1, Article 3(1) and Article 6(2).

4. What is the current ECB reference rate?

   Answer: Not in the sources. The directive says how the reference rate is defined
   (the ECB's most recent main refinancing rate, or the marginal rate from its most
   recent variable-rate tender) and that the applicable rate is the one in force on
   1 January for the first half of the year and on 1 July for the second half. The
   sources do **not** state a current numeric value for that rate. To answer this I
   would need the ECB's published main refinancing operations rate for the relevant
   semester. Source 1, Article 2(7) and Article 3(2).

## Extra note for the owner

- The owner also asked whether a customer may sign for payment within 90 days
  between businesses. The directive caps the contractual payment period at
  **60 calendar days** unless otherwise expressly agreed in the contract, and only if
  the longer term is not grossly unfair to the creditor; an agreed term can be
  treated as unenforceable or as a claim for damages when it is grossly unfair
  (including terms that exclude interest or recovery-cost compensation).
  Source 1, Article 3(5), Article 7(1), 7(2) and 7(3).
